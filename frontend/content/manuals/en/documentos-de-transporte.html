What Araguaney gives you, and what you have to fill in

Araguaney knows what is in your boxes and how much they weigh, because you recorded it box by box. What it does not know — and does not pretend to know — is what your country's authorities require. This guide draws that line clearly, so you know exactly what you bring to your customs broker and what is theirs to handle.

Panel › Help › Transport documents

Araguaney is software, not a foundation and not a tax adviser. It issues no tax documents, files nothing with any customs authority, and interprets no country's tax regime. It generates your cargo data and prints it alongside the details you captured about your own organization.

That is not a limitation we are apologizing for: it is what lets the tool work the same in Mexico, in Colombia, or wherever it is needed next year.

What the platform produces

Three documents per shipment

1

Manifest / packing list

The full detail: every pallet, every box, with product, batch, expiry, quantity and weight. It is the document that proves what the shipment contains and who prepared it. Available as PDF to print and as Excel for whoever needs it in columns.

2

Goods declaration

The summary whoever moves the cargo asks for: which goods are travelling (grouped by product, not box by box), how much they weigh, how many packages, where they leave from and where they are going, and who is dispatching. Available as Excel and JSON, in case your carrier or broker feeds it into their system.

At the top of the file you get the list of missing data. If it says the gross weight is missing, no pallet was weighed; if it says your tax identification is missing, it has not been captured in your center.

3

Box and pallet labels

With their QR codes, so anyone along the chain can see the details without opening anything. The pallet label also carries the humanitarian donation, no commercial value statement.

What you fill in

Your organization's details

Captured once, under Centers, and they appear on every document. We print them as-is: we neither validate nor interpret them.

1

Legal name

Your organization's legal name, which is rarely the same as the name the center operates under. Documents carry the legal one.

2

Tax identification

Whatever your country uses: RFC, RIF, NIT, EIN, any of them. We deliberately do not check the format — validating one country's would break every other.

3

Address

Of the center dispatching the shipment. It is the origin that travels on the declaration.

4

Tariff code for your products (optional)

In the catalog, each product type can carry its HS code (Harmonized System), the one nearly 200 countries use at customs. If you load it, it travels on the declaration and saves your broker work. If not, the document flags it as missing.

Not ours to handle

Take this to your broker

Who issues the transport document and under what rules depends on your country, on who moves the cargo, and on the type of transfer. Your customs broker, your carrier or your accountant resolves that — we give them the data.

If your country requires a specific complement or format, tell us. We can add a profile that translates the declaration's field names into that format. What we will not do is interpret the rules for you: a wrong tax figure coming out of our system is worse than a missing one, because nobody double-checks it.

A donation having no commercial value does not by itself exempt it from anything. Ask a professional in your country what applies in your case; the answer changes with where you are and who is transporting.
