How to build a pallet that arrives in one piece

A badly built pallet does not collapse in your warehouse: it collapses on the airport ramp, or it fails to clear the X-ray arch and has to be broken down right there, with the aircraft waiting. This is what to know before you start stacking.

Panel › Help › Palletizing

The rule people forget most: height is measured from the floor, not from the first box. A standard wooden pallet has already eaten about 15 cm before you put anything on it. If the shipment declares a 160 cm profile, you have roughly 145 cm of cargo.

The second one: no box may overhang the edge. A box hanging over loses much of its compression strength, because it stops resting on the one below, and it is the first thing a forklift catches.

Before stacking

How tall it can be

The shipment declares a height profile and Araguaney warns you when a pallet does not fit. That warning arrives once the pallet is built, so it helps to know beforehand.

1

Lower deck — 160 cm

The most restrictive and the most common case on commercial passenger flights, which is how most aid travels. It is the hold beneath the cabin of a narrow-body aircraft. With the pallet included, your cargo should not exceed roughly 145 cm.

2

X-ray arch — 170 cm

Here is the catch: the handler's arch can be lower than the aircraft door. It is no use fitting the hold if the pallet cannot clear the scanner at the cargo terminal. If you do not know which arch your operator uses, build to the lowest profile.

3

Main deck — 180 cm or more

Only on freighters or the main deck of a combi. If your shipment goes that way you have room, but confirm it with whoever coordinates the flight before building tall: bringing a 180 cm pallet down on the ramp is half an hour of work and damaged boxes.

How to stack

Order matters more than technique

1

Heavy at the bottom, always

Cans and liquids at the base; biscuits, diapers and clothing on top. It is not only so the light items are not crushed: a pallet with its weight high tips over on a forklift turn.

2

Straight columns, not brickwork

Stacking box on box, corner to corner, holds considerably more weight than interlocking them like a brick wall: the force travels down the corners of the cardboard, which is where cardboard is strong. The interlocked pattern looks firmer and holds less. If you interlock for lateral stability, do it only on the top layer.

3

Nothing past the edge

Not a centimetre. If your boxes do not match the pallet's footprint, leaving a gap in the middle is better than letting them hang outside.

4

A flat layer before you continue

If a layer ends up uneven, the next one starts crooked and never straightens itself out. Fix it before going up, not after.

How to secure it

Stretch wrap, corner boards and strapping

1

Anchor the wrap to the pallet

The first turns take in the wooden base, not just the bottom boxes. If the wrap does not grip the pallet, the whole load slides off it and all that work was wasted.

2

Spiral up, overlapping

Each turn covers half of the previous one, up and back down. Add extra turns at the top and bottom, where the stress concentrates. Wrap holds; it does not protect: never use it to hide a bad stack.

3

Corner boards on tall or heavy pallets

Cardboard profiles on the four corners spread the strap pressure and keep it from cutting into the boxes. On a tall pallet they are the difference between arriving intact and arriving with the corners crushed.

4

Strap over the corner board, never bare

A tight strap against bare cardboard cuts it. And a loose strap is worth nothing: if you can fit your hand between the strap and the load, it is loose.

Before closing it

The last checks

Weigh it and measure it. When you close the pallet in Araguaney you can record the scale weight and the height. That weight is the one that travels on the documents, and that height is what gets compared against the shipment's profile.

The label goes visible and flat. If the QR ends up creased under the wrap or facing the wall, somebody will have to break the pallet down to read it.

If something does not add up, say so. A pallet that ended up 10 cm taller than it should is not a problem until it reaches the airport. There it is, and there there is no time.
